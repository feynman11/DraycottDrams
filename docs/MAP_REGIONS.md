# Map: Scotch whisky region shading

## Data source and approach

**No open, ready-made polygon set of the Scotch whisky regions exists.** A search of GitHub (repository
and code search), Wikimedia Commons, OpenStreetMap and UK open-data portals in October 2026 found only:

- Distillery *point* datasets (University of Edinburgh DataShare, VisitScotland fixtures), not region
  boundaries.
- Static region maps as PDF/SVG (Wikimedia Commons `Scotch regions.svg`, Society of Wine Educators
  map, Collins pictorial map), none with usable geometry and mostly under non-commercial or
  unspecified licences.
- No OpenStreetMap relations for the whisky regions (they are not administrative areas).

The regions are, however, legally defined in terms of council areas and wards by
[Regulation 10 of the Scotch Whisky Regulations 2009](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2009/2890/regulation/10/made):

| Region / locality | Definition (reg. 10(5)–(7)) |
| --- | --- |
| Campbeltown | the South Kintyre ward of Argyll and Bute |
| Islay | the Isle of Islay |
| Speyside | the eight Moray Council wards (i.e. the whole of Moray) plus the Badenoch and Strathspey ward of Highland |
| Highland | everything north of the Highland line |
| Lowland | everything south of it |

The Highland line runs along the southern foreshore of the Firth of Clyde to Greenock, to Cardross
Station, east to Earl's Seat in the Campsie Fells, east to the Wallace Monument, along the B998/A91 to
Milnathort, the M90 to Bridge of Earn, the River Earn to the Tay, and the southern foreshore of the
Tay to the North Sea.

**Chosen approach: derive the polygons from Natural Earth council areas.**
`lib/whisky-regions.json` was generated by a one-off script from
[Natural Earth 10m "Admin 1 – States, Provinces"](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/10m-cultural-vectors/)
(**public domain**, no attribution required), which carries all 32 Scottish council areas:

1. Whole councils are assigned to a region; the four the Highland line crosses (Stirling,
   Clackmannanshire, Perth and Kinross, West Dunbartonshire) are cut along a hand-digitised version
   of the line described above (about 30 vertices through the named places).
2. Campbeltown is the Kintyre peninsula south of ~55.56°N (South Kintyre ward: Campbeltown, Southend,
   Drumlemble, Peninver, Bellochantuy; Carradale, just north, is in the next ward).
3. Badenoch and Strathspey is a hand-drawn ward approximation intersected with the Highland council
   polygon (Laggan/Dalwhinnie up to Carrbridge/Grantown; Tomatin stays in Highland). Its east and south
   edges are the real Moray/Aberdeenshire/Perth borders from Natural Earth.
4. "Islands" (not a legal region, but the grouping the club's data uses as `Island`) is Arran, Bute,
   Jura, Mull and the other Argyll islands except Islay; Skye, Raasay and the Small Isles; the Western
   Isles; and Orkney. Shetland (no distillery) is left unshaded.
5. The six regions are unioned, simplified with topology preserved (so shared borders simplify
   identically and leave no slivers), rings under 3 km² are dropped and coordinates rounded to 4 dp.
   Result: 6 features, ~3,900 vertices, 70 KB.

The result is accurate to Natural Earth's 10m coastline (roughly 100–500 m) and reads correctly from
country level to about zoom 9. It is not legally precise: the two ward cuts and the Highland line are
approximations good to a few kilometres on uninhabited ground. Known consequences: Dalwhinnie is
shaded Speyside (as the regulation has it, although it markets itself as Highland); Glengoyne sits on
the Highland line; coastal pins such as Jura and Glenmorangie can sit a few hundred metres outside
the fill at high zoom because their stored coordinates are offshore of the 10m coastline.

To regenerate, rerun the conversion against a fresh Natural Earth download (the script is kept out
of the repo; it needs `@turf/turf`, `topojson-server`, `topojson-simplify` and `topojson-client`).

## How it is wired

- `lib/whisky-regions.ts`: typed access to the GeoJSON, legend order, and the per-region fill colours
  (OKLCH on hues from `styles/globals.css`, stored as hex because MapLibre cannot parse `oklch()`).
- `components/whisky/map-regions.ts`: adds the GeoJSON source with a `fill` (opacity 0.14) and a
  hairline `line` layer beneath the basemap's first symbol layer, and toggles their `visibility`.
- `components/whisky/map-visualization.tsx`: the "Shade Scotland's regions" option (on by default, not
  persisted, like the other map options) and the region swatches in the Legend panel.
